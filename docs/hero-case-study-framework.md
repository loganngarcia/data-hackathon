# Hero Case Study Framework

## Purpose

Once the outputs exist, we need three hero nonprofits that make the product story undeniable:

- a resilient leader,
- a tipping-risk case,
- and a realistic intervention candidate.

## Selection Criteria

Choose cases that score well on at least three of these dimensions:

- Clear signal strength
- Easy-to-explain drivers
- Visually clean trend history
- Strong contrast with peer group
- Good fit for the demo storyline
- Low ambiguity in the underlying data

## Hero Types

### Resilient Leader

- Use a nonprofit with strong reserves, stable or growing revenue, and low volatility.
- The story should show why the organization is resilient, not just that it ranks high.
- Best when the trend line is clean and the explanation is short.

### Tipping-Risk Case

- Use a nonprofit with clear deterioration or high fragility.
- Prefer a case with visible warning signals and a believable near-term risk narrative.
- The story should show how the model flags stress before a collapse happens.

### Intervention Candidate

- Use a nonprofit where one or two scenario levers change the outlook in a meaningful way.
- Prefer a case where the recommended intervention is intuitive to a judge.
- The story should show a before/after shift that supports action.

## Selection Workflow

1. Rank candidate nonprofits by score and by explanation clarity.
2. Filter out cases with messy data, missing histories, or confusing edge conditions.
3. Compare each candidate against peer organizations.
4. Pick the case with the strongest story, not just the strongest number.
5. Verify that all three cases support the same overall thesis.

## Final Sanity Check

- Can we explain the case in one sentence?
- Does the chart tell the story without extra narration?
- Does the case help a judge understand the product in under 30 seconds?
- Does the case avoid unsupported causal claims?
