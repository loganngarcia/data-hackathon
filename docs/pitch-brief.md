## Three Hero Case Studies

### Ocean Bridge Coalition -- The Resilient Leader

**Profile:** Coastal resilience nonprofit, San Diego, CA. Score 86 (Steady). Revenue $12.5M, growth +14.2%, reserve coverage 11.4 months, 86 staff.

**Why it is strong:**
- Revenue has compounded for three consecutive years without a matching staffing spike, meaning growth is not being consumed by overhead.
- Liquid reserves cover almost a year of expenses, well above the peer median of 7.2 months.
- No single funding source exceeds 45% of total revenue. The HHI across revenue streams is low. This is a diversified operation.
- The revenue mix leans toward earned income (42% program fees), which is more predictable than grant-dependent revenue.

**Peer comparison:** Ahead on reserve months (11.4 vs. 7.2), ahead on revenue growth (14.2% vs. 6.0%), more efficient on staffing (6.9 per $1M vs. 8.1).

**Scenario:** Scale the earned revenue program -- increase by 12% while holding hiring flat. Projected reserves rise to 13.1 months. Risk band shifts from Steady to Foundation. The memo can justify action with concrete benchmark deltas.

**What to say:** "This is the organization where targeted support has the highest return on impact. The operating story is clean, the peer frame confirms it, and the scenario shows the growth path is sustainable. This is your 'support now' case."

### Bright Path Youth -- The Watch Case

**Profile:** Youth services nonprofit, Sacramento, CA. Score 72 (Watch). Revenue $5.4M, growth +4.8%, reserve coverage 6.1 months, 42 staff.

**Why it needs stabilization:**
- Program demand is rising and revenue is growing, but reserve rebuilding has not kept pace. The organization is running hot without building cushion.
- Reserve coverage (6.1 months) sits just below the peer median (6.8 months). Not critical, but trending in the wrong direction.
- Payroll pressure is absorbing gains that could otherwise strengthen the balance sheet. Staff per $1M is 7.8 vs. peer median 7.5 -- slightly above, and climbing.
- Individual giving is rising fast enough to matter, but only if it converts to recurring support.

**Peer comparison:** Slightly below on reserves (6.1 vs. 6.8), slightly below on growth (4.8% vs. 5.1%), slightly above on staffing cost (7.8 vs. 7.5).

**Scenario:** Stabilize the program footprint -- hold headcount flat, direct incremental funding into reserve rebuilding. Projected reserves rise to 7.2 months (back to peer median). Risk band shifts from Watch to Steady. The recommendation is operational: stabilize first, then expand.

**What to say:** "Bright Path is not in crisis, but it is drifting. The answer is not to slash growth or celebrate it blindly. It is to hold the line on headcount, rebuild reserves, and keep the mission intact. The numbers support a stabilization-first plan."

### Harbor House Network -- The Tipping-Risk Case

**Profile:** Housing stability nonprofit, Oakland, CA. Score 54 (At Risk). Revenue $8.1M, growth -3.1%, reserve coverage 3.8 months, 59 staff.

**Why it is fragile:**
- Revenue is contracting (-3.1%) while peers are growing (+2.7%). The gap is widening.
- Reserve coverage is 3.8 months against a peer median of 6.5. For a housing-services mission that cannot tolerate service disruption, this is critically thin.
- Revenue concentration is dangerously high: 47% government grants and 28% from a single foundation partner. If either source shifts, the organization has almost no buffer.
- The filing shows the classic tipping-risk pattern: declining revenue, eroding reserves, and high concentration, all in the same year.

**Peer comparison:** Below on reserves (3.8 vs. 6.5), below on growth (-3.1% vs. +2.7%), staffing efficiency is acceptable (7.3 vs. 7.9) but irrelevant if revenue continues to fall.

**Scenario:** Protect core programs -- redirect flexibility toward housing services and cash conservation. Projected reserves inch up to 4.4 months. Risk band remains At Risk. Even the best intervention does not move Harbor House out of the danger zone in one year.

**What to say:** "This is the cautionary case. Harbor House is not failing its mission -- it is operating with too little cushion for the volatility already visible in the filing. The memo says: protect continuity, conserve cash, and diversify the grant base. This organization needs sustained structural support, not a one-time grant."

## Likely Judge Questions and Suggested Answers

### Q1: How do you define resilience? Is the score just a financial health metric?

Resilience is not the same as financial health. A healthy nonprofit might still be fragile if its revenue is concentrated or its reserves are thin relative to its obligations. Our score captures eight signals that together measure the ability to absorb shocks and sustain operations, not just current profitability. The distinction matters: an organization can be running a surplus and still be one grant loss away from crisis.

### Q2: Why these eight signals? How did you choose the weights?

The eight signals cover the dimensions that the nonprofit finance literature and practitioner experience identify as predictive of organizational durability: growth trajectory, volatility, cost discipline, liquidity, funding diversification, balance sheet trend, workforce stability, and deficit history. We double-weight concentration risk and liquidity because our exploratory analysis showed they are the strongest differentiators between organizations that survive funding shocks and those that do not. The weights are transparent and adjustable -- an advisor could tune them to match their own risk framework.

### Q3: How reliable is 990 data? Are you concerned about data quality?

990 data is messy. Schema drift across tax years changes field names. Amended returns create duplicates. Missing values are common, especially in 990-EZ filings. We handle this with an XPath alias map for schema resolution, deduplication to keep only the latest filing per org-year, and a strict no-imputation policy for financial fields. Organizations need at least four years of data to receive a score. We would rather have a smaller, trustworthy panel than a large, noisy one.

### Q4: What happens if a key grant disappears? Can you simulate that?

Yes. The scenario engine supports controlled intervention levers including revenue diversification, reserve grants, margin improvement, and staffing changes. For Harbor House, we simulated a defensive scenario and showed that even the best available intervention does not move the organization out of the At Risk band in one year. That is an honest result -- and it is the kind of answer an advisor needs to hear.

### Q5: How is this different from what ProPublica or GuideStar already provide?

ProPublica and GuideStar surface individual 990 filings. They are research tools, not decision tools. Tipping Point adds three things they do not offer: a composite resilience score that synthesizes seven years of filings into one comparable number, a peer benchmarking frame that puts each organization in context, and a scenario simulator that lets an advisor test interventions before committing resources. The workflow goes from screening to recommendation in three clicks.

### Q6: Could this be used at scale with all nonprofits in the IRS database?

The architecture supports it. The pipeline processes XML filings in batch and writes static JSON artifacts. Scaling from hundreds to tens of thousands of organizations is a data volume problem, not an architectural redesign. The scoring model, peer benchmarking, and scenario engine all operate per-organization and parallelize naturally. The main constraint is data availability -- not every organization has seven years of clean filings.

### Q7: What would you build next if you had more time?

Three things. First, integrate Federal Audit Clearinghouse data to add audit findings and questioned costs as additional risk signals. Second, build a proper time-series early-warning model that predicts tipping risk two years forward, trained on the historical crisis events we identified. Third, add geographic and sector drill-downs to the screener so an advisor can filter by county, NTEE code, or revenue band before scanning the portfolio.

### Q8: How do you handle the copilot? Is it just GPT generating text?

The copilot is constrained to four intents: explain why flagged, summarize trajectory, recommend intervention, and draft memo. Every intent consumes structured evidence objects from the scoring pipeline, not raw data. The prompts are grounded in the same numbers the frontend displays. And every intent has a deterministic template fallback, so the app works identically if the LLM is unavailable. We built it this way because judges should never see a hallucinated recommendation.
